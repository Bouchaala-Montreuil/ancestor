Ancestor is a training record for people whose week does not start on Monday. It decides what to do today from what you actually lifted, then shows you the working — the sets, the effort, the recovery estimate, and the assumptions inside it.

01

The argument

Most training apps are slot machines. They measure the wrong thing, in public, with someone else's data.

Streaks

A streak measures compliance, not adaptation.

Seven days in a row says you opened an app. It does not say you recovered, and it cannot tell you that a fourth heavy squat session this week is a mistake.

Decisions

A plan that cannot say why is a guess.

Today is TRAIN, MODIFY or REST, with the reasons listed: last session's effort, the muscle's recovery estimate, your declared sleep, your limitations.

Ownership

Your record should outlive the server.

No account, no sync, no telemetry. The log is a file on your phone that you can export, print on A4, and still read in ten years.

02

Six tabs, one job each

No home feed, no recommendations, no social layer. Every tab answers one question and then stops.

Today

What do I do today?

A decision, not a prompt. Training readiness from the log: the muscle groups still recovering, the load you have actually been handling, and the sessions you have missed. Then, if the answer is train, the session it recommends.

Session

Log it in one action per set.

Weight, reps, effort — one press each, large targets, a rest timer that keeps running when the phone locks. A draft survives an interruption, because a session logged in a gym basement is a session logged without signal.

Body

The muscle is the unit of recovery.

Front and back views, twelve muscle groups, tap to select. Each one carries its last recorded work, the exercises that contribute to it, and a recovery estimate with every factor and multiplier written out.

Progress

The chart has an axis you can read.

Estimated 1RM and volume over time, plus a personal-record board. Every plotted value is one you can trace back to a set you logged.

Science

Eleven lessons, and the references.

Effort, proximity to failure, volume, frequency, progression, protein. Written to be read once, with citations you can check, and an explicit statement of what the evidence cannot settle.

Profile

Yours to leave with.

Goal, equipment, limitations, units, language. Export the whole record as JSON, import it on a new phone, or erase it — one button, no confirmation theatre, no copy retained anywhere, because there is nowhere to retain it.

03

The system

One direction, five tokens, two typefaces, and a drawing grammar that every diagram and chart obeys. It is documented, not remembered.

04

The record leaves the phone

An app that only exists on a screen is a rental. Ancestor prints an A4 training record from its own engine.

The sheet is generated by the same functions that draw the screen — no screenshot, no second layout. Session log, load and volume, personal records, the recovery reasoning, and a footer that says what the page cannot prove.

It is also how the app is tested: if the printed record and the screen disagree, one of them is lying, and the tests say which.

06

What it does not do

The honest list. It is shorter than most, and it is inside the product as well as on this page.

  1. A training atlas, not a clinical model

    The anatomy is a real, licensed mesh — 221 meshes, 218 named muscles, 904 KB, CC BY-SA 4.0 — rotatable in the app, and projected into the flat figure when you want to tap a region instead of spin a body. What it is not: a medical model. It has no tissue detail, no pathology, no individual variation, and its colours are training estimates, never measured activation.

  2. An installable PWA, not a store app

    It installs from the browser and runs offline. There is no signed build in a store; that is a distribution decision, not a design one, and it is stated in the About screen.

  3. No cloud, so no sync

    One phone, one file. Export and import are the whole of the migration story. The UI never says “synced”, because nothing is.

  4. Recovery is an estimate, not a measurement

    It is computed from logged work and declared sleep, and every factor is printed next to the number. There is no wearable and no claim to one.

  5. It has not been photographed on a device yet

    Every screen here was drawn from markup and verified by rendering the real DOM. Type overflow at 320 px is checked; how it feels in a gym is not.
